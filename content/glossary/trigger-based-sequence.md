A **trigger-based sequence** is an automated series of outreach messages initiated by a specific event or date in an individual client's record — not by a calendar date chosen for the whole list.

## Trigger-based versus batch

A **batch campaign** sends the same message to everyone on Tuesday. A **trigger-based sequence** sends a message to each client at the right moment relative to *their* circumstances: 60 days before *their* x-date, three days after *their* quote was presented, the week *their* teen driver turns sixteen.

Batch campaigns are easy to build and produce the response rates you would expect from mail that is obviously not about the recipient. Triggered sequences are relevant by construction.

## The canonical renewal sequence

The pattern most independent agencies should run, timed off the **x-date**:

- **Day −60** — proactive review offer, before any renewal notice arrives
- **Day −30** — renewal terms and any rate change, explained rather than mailed
- **Day −14** — direct check-in from the assigned producer or CSR
- **Day −7** — final confirmation and a clear next step

The −60 touch does most of the work. It establishes contact before the client has any reason to feel defensive and long before a competitor's ad finds them.

## What makes a sequence work

- **A reliable trigger.** Garbage x-dates mean the sequence fires at the wrong time or never.
- **Suppression rules.** Anyone who replies, calls, or binds must exit immediately. Nothing destroys credibility faster than a "just checking in" email sent after the client already renewed.
- **Multi-channel** delivery, since email alone reaches a fraction of a book.
- **A real reply path.** Automation opens the conversation; a human has to be able to continue it.

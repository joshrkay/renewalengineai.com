A **comparative rater** is software that takes one set of applicant information and returns indicative quotes from multiple carriers at once. Instead of re-keying the same driver, vehicle, and coverage data into six carrier portals, the producer enters it once and compares results side by side.

## Where raters are strongest

Comparative rating works best where risks are standardized and rating algorithms are well-defined — which in practice means **personal lines**: auto, homeowners, renters, and umbrella. Commercial lines rating involves far more judgment, more carrier-specific appetite, and more underwriter involvement, so rater coverage there is thinner and less reliable.

## What a rater does not do

A rater returns a price. It does not tell you:

- Whether the carrier will actually accept the risk after underwriting review
- Whether the coverage forms are genuinely comparable at the quoted limits
- Whether the client will still be there when the quote is presented

That last one is the operational gap. A rater compresses the *quoting* step to minutes and leaves the *follow-up* step entirely manual — which is why agencies with excellent rating tools still lose quoted business at high rates.

## The integration question

The valuable configuration is rater and **AMS** connected: applicant data flows from the AMS into the rater, and the bound policy flows back without re-entry. Where that link is missing, the rater creates a second silo of client data, and the agency now has two systems disagreeing about the same account — an outcome that makes downstream automation harder, not easier.

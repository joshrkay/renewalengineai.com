**Account rounding** is the practice of systematically adding policies to existing clients — moving a household from monoline to multi-line, or a commercial account from a single coverage to a full program.

## Why it outperforms new-business acquisition

For an existing client, the agency already has the relationship, the underwriting data, and the trust. There is no acquisition cost and no credibility to establish. And the result improves two metrics simultaneously: revenue per client rises, and **retention rate** rises because **policies per client** rises.

New business does one of those things. Account rounding does both.

## The trigger events worth automating

Cross-sell works when it is timely and specific, and fails when it is a generic blast. The events that reliably signal opportunity:

- A **renewal** approaching on the one policy you hold
- A **home purchase or move** (public records, mortgage change, address update)
- A **new driver** in the household — a teen reaching driving age
- A **life event**: marriage, new child, retirement
- A **business change** on a commercial account: new location, new vehicle, headcount growth
- A **coverage gap** visible in the data — an auto client with no umbrella and high liability limits

## Why it does not happen without a system

Every agency knows account rounding matters. Most do it sporadically, because it depends on someone noticing the right moment for the right client among thousands — which is precisely the kind of continuous, low-salience monitoring humans are worst at and software is best at.

The systematic version is a standing query over the book that surfaces triggered opportunities weekly, with the specific reason attached, ready for a producer to act on. Not a campaign. A queue.

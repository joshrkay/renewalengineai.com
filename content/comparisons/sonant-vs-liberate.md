> **TL;DR:** Sonant AI and Liberate both put conversational AI on insurance phone lines, but they are built for different buyers. Sonant is an AI receptionist priced and packaged for independent agencies — it answers routine calls, takes quote intake, and writes notes back to the AMS, with published tiers starting around $299/month. Liberate positions itself as a "System of Action" for insurance — an always-on AI agent (Nicole) automating voice, SMS, email, and digital interactions across service, claims/FNOL, and sales, sold to carriers, MGAs, and larger distribution organizations at enterprise scale. If you are a 3-15 person independent agency whose pain is the phone, evaluate Sonant first. If you are a carrier, MGA, or large multi-office operation automating whole service and claims workflows, Liberate is playing your game.

Almost every "Sonant vs. Liberate" question we hear is really a question about organizational size and scope. The two products overlap on one visible feature — an AI that answers an insurance phone call competently — and diverge on nearly everything around it.

## What each one actually is

**Sonant AI** is an AI receptionist built specifically for P&C insurance agencies, brokers, and distributors. It answers routine inbound calls, recognizes callers, handles quote intake and servicing questions in insurance vocabulary, schedules appointments, transfers what it shouldn't handle to a human, and writes post-call summaries back into the management system. Its published integrations run across the agency stack: EZLynx, Applied Epic, HawkSoft, QQCatalyst, AMS360, Zywave, InsuredMine, and AgencyZoom.

**Liberate** describes itself as the insurance industry's first "System of Action" — an always-on AI agent it calls Nicole that automates interactions across voice, SMS, email, and digital channels, spanning policy service, claims intake (FNOL), and sales workflows. Its named integrations skew enterprise: Guidewire PolicyCenter and ClaimCenter, Duck Creek Claims, Applied Epic, AMS360, and Salesforce — alongside a strategic partnership with HawkSoft that brings its voice AI to HawkSoft agencies for service, quoting, and call routing. The company announced a $50M raise at a $300M valuation in late 2025 and reported passing $100B in premiums processed in March 2026 — signals of where it plays: at scale.

## When Sonant is the better fit

- You are an independent agency and the bottleneck is specifically the **phone**: missed calls, after-hours voicemail, front-desk overload.
- You want published, agency-sized pricing you can approve without a procurement process.
- Your AMS is one of the eight agency systems it lists, and you want call notes landing there automatically.
- You want one job done well now, not a platform program.

## When Liberate is the better fit

- You are a **carrier, MGA, or large multi-office distributor** with service and claims volume that justifies an enterprise AI deployment.
- FNOL and claims workflows are in scope, not just reception.
- You need one AI layer across voice, SMS, email, and digital, wired into policy-admin systems like Guidewire or Duck Creek.
- You are a HawkSoft agency drawn by the official partnership — that is Liberate's clearest downmarket path, and worth evaluating against Sonant's HawkSoft integration on your own call volume.

## What neither one does

Neither product runs your renewal book for you. An AI receptionist answers the calls that come in; a system of action automates the workflows you configure. The renewal problem — knowing which of your policies expire in 60 days, which are at risk, and touching every one of them with personalized outreach before the X-date — is a data-and-operations problem that sits behind the phone, in your AMS. That layer is what [renewal automation](/resources/ai-renewal-automation-playbook) means, and it complements either tool: several stacks we design pair a voice AI on inbound with a renewal engine on outbound.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a small independent agency buy Liberate?

Liberate's positioning and integration set skew enterprise, but its HawkSoft partnership gives HawkSoft agencies a real path to its voice AI. If you are on HawkSoft, get quotes from both; if you are on another agency system, Sonant's published tiers will usually be the faster evaluation.

### Do these replace a CSR?

Neither vendor pitches full replacement, and we would not either. They absorb routine call volume so licensed staff spend time on work that needs judgment. The arithmetic on that trade is in [AI tools vs. hiring a CSR](/resources/ai-tools-vs-hiring-a-csr).
